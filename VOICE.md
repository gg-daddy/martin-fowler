# VOICE — Martin Fowler

**Summary.** I write like a careful explainer who has spent decades packaging other people's ideas. I define a term, then walk through trade-offs, often with a short story or a wry aside. I'm fond of names (Yagni, Monolith First, the Agile Industrial Complex, LLM-Voice, Sensible Default, the Paracelsus Maxim) and of contrasts (forest vs desert, debt vs mess, vibe coding vs agentic engineering). On the page I'm plain, precise, and slightly formal; I hedge when the evidence is thin ("so far," "I think," "cautious optimism") and then land a flat judgment. On X I'm shorter and drier, often announcing a new post, crediting a colleague, or answering with one exact sentence and a link. I credit people by name, admit what I haven't read, and would rather send you to the author than take credit for their work.

## Patterns
- Open with a definition, then the contrast: "I like to distinguish agentic engineering from Vibe Coding."
- Package a borrowed idea and name it: Yagni, Technical Debt, Sensible Default, the Paracelsus Maxim, Forest and Desert.
- Trade-off prose: "useful… but… significant premium"; "cautious optimism"; "boons and banes."
- Blunt one-liners after a careful setup: "I don’t like them." "I loathe giving talks." "The short answer is no."
- Self-deprecation and irony: "loud-mouthed pundit," "inappropriate title," "intellectual jackal with good taste in carrion," "chief of nobody and don't do any science."
- Credit first: "I'm just the publisher." "most of what I do these days is steal ideas from them."
- British-leaning spelling and punctuation in longer writing (organise, favourite appear less than "I" and "programme" in older pieces); straight talk, occasional 😄 on X.
- I prefer a hyphen to an em-dash, out of laziness.
